# 1. Purpose and decision rule

This document sets the criteria that human annotators use to decide whether a YouTube or TikTok video belongs in the dataset on **safe restraint and immobilization techniques for infants and young children**. The criteria are anchored in published professional guidance (section 2), so that each borderline decision can be traced to a source.

**Decision rule.** A video is labeled **Relevant** only if all three conditions in section 4 are met:

- **A. Age:** the child is an infant or young child (birth to about 5 years).
- **B. Topic:** the video is about holding, restricting or immobilizing a child's movement for care.
- **C. Content:** the video shows or explains how it is done, or how to do it safely.

If any condition fails, the label is **Irrelevant**. If the annotator cannot decide after following the watching procedure (section 8), the label is **Unsure**, with a reason and a note. Unsure items are resolved by adjudication (section 10); they are never guessed.

Scope is medical, dental, imaging and trauma care. Vehicle child restraints (car seats, harnesses) are out of scope.

# 2. Basis in published guidelines

No single guideline covers this topic. Guidance is split by clinical setting, so the criteria combine the sources below. The "Where used" column shows which part of the criteria each source supports.

| Source | What it says that matters here | Where used |
|---|---|---|
| Royal College of Nursing (2019), *Restrictive physical intervention and the clinical holding of children and young people: guidance for nursing staff* | Defines clinical holding as using limited force to hold a child still for a procedure, still counted as a restrictive physical intervention. Holding without assent is a last resort. The need for holding is reduced through information, distraction, analgesia and sedation. Use the least restrictive option for the minimum time, with age-appropriate techniques such as wrapping and splinting. | Definitions (3); conditions B and C; unsafe demonstration rule (9) |
| American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry, *Use of Protective Stabilization for Pediatric Dental Patients* (Reference Manual, latest revision 2024) | Treats protective stabilization as an advanced behavior guidance technique. Covers immobilization techniques and equipment, indications, contraindications, consent and assent, monitoring during restriction of movement, and alternatives such as deferral or pharmacological techniques. | Dental subtopic; definition of protective stabilization |
| AAP and AAPD, *Guidelines for Monitoring and Management of Pediatric Patients Before, During, and After Sedation for Diagnostic and Therapeutic Procedures* (Pediatr Dent 2025;47(6):E100-E128) | Immobilization devices must be applied so that they avoid airway obstruction and chest restriction. Head position and respiratory movement are checked frequently, a hand or foot stays exposed, and the child is never left unattended. | Safety signals in condition C; unsafe demonstration rule (9) |
| Taddio et al. (HELPinKIDS), *Reducing the pain of childhood vaccination: an evidence-based clinical practice guideline* (CMAJ 2010;182:1989, summary) | Hold or position children upright instead of supine for injections, in a comfortable parent hold such as a bear hug or lap. Excessive restraint may increase distress. | Needle-procedure subtopic; parent comfort holds |
| ACS COT, ACEP and NAEMSP (2018), *Spinal Motion Restriction in the Trauma Patient: a joint position statement* (Prehosp Emerg Care) | Prefers the term spinal motion restriction (SMR) to immobilization. Lists indications, a properly fitted collar, limited time on backboards, and padding under a child's shoulders because of the proportionally larger head. Age alone should not decide use. | Trauma and spinal subtopic; definition of SMR |
| Two 2024 scoping reviews: *The use and prevention of procedural restraint in children* (J Pediatr Nurs 2024;79:e110-e118) and *Holding and restraining children for clinical procedures* (J Child Health Care 2024) | The first found that research lacked definitions of restraint and that no evidence exists on preventing restraint. The second found 14 different terms for holding or restraining a child across 30 papers. | Plain functional definitions (3); wide vocabulary in the search |

The terminology problem in the last row is the reason for section 3: the same act is called holding, restraint, stabilization, immobilization or motion restriction depending on the specialty, and videos use all of these words.

# 3. Definitions

These working definitions are written for annotators. They follow the sources in section 2 but are simplified and are the project's own wording.

| Term | Working definition | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Placing a child in a posture for comfort or access while the child is free to move away | Lying on an exam table, sitting upright and unsecured |
| Comfort or clinical holding | A carer or clinician holds the child still with limited force for a procedure, ideally with assent and the least restriction (RCN) | Bear hug for an injection, parent-held toddler for an ear exam, gentle hands-on containment of a newborn for a procedure |
| Swaddling and wrapping | Wrapping an infant in a blanket so that the limbs are contained, for a procedure | Swaddling for a heel prick, wrapping for an MRI scan |
| Protective stabilization | Dental term for restricting movement during treatment, including devices (AAPD) | Papoose board, partial wrap in a dental chair |
| Immobilization device | Equipment that limits movement of a body part or of the whole child | Papoose board, vacuum immobilizer, limb splint |
| Spinal motion restriction (SMR) | Limiting movement of a potentially injured spine in trauma; the preferred term over immobilization (ACS COT, ACEP, NAEMSP) | Collar and scoop stretcher for an injured child |
| Restraint | Umbrella term used here for any method that limits movement by force, wrapping or a device | All rows above except positioning |

**Positioning versus restraint: the test.** Ask: *could the child move away freely if they wanted to?*

- **Yes.** The child is only positioned. Condition B fails.
- **No**, because a person's hands or body, a wrap, a strap, a board or a device limits movement. This is holding or restraint. Continue to condition C.

A child sitting on a lap with nobody securing the arms or body is positioning. A child in a parent's bear hug that secures the arms and body is holding.

**Age groups.** An **infant** is under 12 months. A **young child** is about 1 to 5 years, so the range covered is birth to about 5 years. Toddlers and preschoolers are included.

# 4. Labels and the three conditions

Every video receives one label: **Relevant**, **Irrelevant** or **Unsure**. Relevant needs all three conditions.

| Condition | Question | Passes when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Age | Is the child an infant or young child (birth to about 5 years)? | See the age rules below | Stated or clearly visible age is about 6 years or older (school-age, teen, adult) |
| B. Topic | Is it about holding, restricting or immobilizing the child's movement for care? | Clinical holding, comfort holding, swaddling or wrapping for a procedure, papoose board, immobilization device, spinal motion restriction, protective stabilization, or alternatives to restraint presented in relation to holding | Positioning only, sleep swaddling, or any exclusion in section 5 |
| C. Content | Does it show or explain how it is done, or how to do it safely? | Demonstration, step-by-step explanation, safety rules, risks, or professional guidance on the technique | Passing mention of a term, advertisement, news item with no technique, visit vlog with no explanation |

**Age rules (condition A).**

1. A **stated age** in the title, caption, description, transcript, on-screen text or speech overrides appearance.
2. If no age is stated, use visual cues: an infant is carried or held in arms, cannot sit unaided, uses a pacifier or bottle, or is clearly small compared with an adult; a toddler or preschooler walks, is small enough to be lifted easily onto a lap, or is shown in toddler or preschool context.
3. **Dolls, manikins and animations** count if they are explicitly presented as demonstrating the technique for an infant or young child.
4. **School-age clarification.** School-age children (about 6 years and older) are out of scope. The words "children", "kids" or "pediatric" without any age are not enough. Look for the cues above. If the child's age cannot be judged, or could be either a young child or a school-age child, label **Unsure** (reason: age unclear). Do not guess from a single ambiguous cue, and do not decide from the face alone.
5. **Mixed ages.** If infants or young children are a substantial part of the video, condition A is met. If they appear only briefly, it is not.

**Mixed content (condition C).** If restraint or immobilization is only part of a longer video (for example a hospital vlog), it meets condition C only when a continuous segment of at least 30 seconds, or about one quarter of the video, shows or explains the technique. For videos shorter than 30 seconds, the whole video is judged.

# 5. Exclusions and false matches

The search terms also match unrelated content. The following are **Irrelevant** (condition B fails), whatever the title says:

| Exclusion | Why it appears in results | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Restraint collapse** (the parenting term for a child holding emotions together at school and melting down at home) | The word "restraint" is shared, the meaning is emotional, not physical | "Why kids fall apart after school: restraint collapse" |
| **Music and entertainment** (songs, lyrics, albums, dance, gaming, ASMR, comedy) that use words such as restraint, immobilize or hold still | Titles and hashtags match, the content is not care | A song or lyric video, a game character "immobilized" |
| **Vehicle child restraints** (car seats, harnesses, strollers) | The word "child restraint" is used for cars | Car seat installation |
| **Sleep swaddling** or soothing | Swaddling is not for a procedure | Swaddling tutorial for bedtime |
| **Non-medical or adult restraint** (martial arts, security, police, bondage, animal handling) | Shared vocabulary | Self-defense holds |
| **Behavioral restraint** in schools or mental health settings, not tied to a care procedure | Different setting | Physical intervention for aggression in class |
| **Videos unavailable at labeling time** | Deleted or private after collection | Label **Unsure**, reason: video unavailable |

# 6. Borderline cases

The "Basis" column names the source for the rule. Where the sources do not settle a case, it says "project decision", and the case is tested in the pilot.

| Case | Label | Reason | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent bear hug or upright lap hold for a vaccine, with explanation | Relevant | Holding for a procedure, with explanation | CMAJ 2010; RCN 2019 |
| Breastfeeding or sugar water for vaccine pain, no holding explained | Irrelevant | Pain relief only; fails B | Project decision |
| Child sits upright on a lap with nobody securing the body | Irrelevant | Positioning only (section 3 test) | Project decision |
| Swaddling an infant for MRI or a heel prick | Relevant | Wrapping to contain limbs for a procedure | RCN 2019 (wrapping and splinting) |
| Papoose board in a dental chair, technique or safety explained | Relevant | Protective stabilization | AAPD |
| Dental video of a crying child with no explanation | Irrelevant | Fails C | Project decision |
| Collar, scoop stretcher or backboard on a child in trauma care | Relevant | Spinal motion restriction | ACS COT, ACEP, NAEMSP 2018 |
| Same, but adult patient only | Irrelevant | Fails A | Project decision |
| Splinting a young child's arm fracture, focused on immobilizing the limb | Relevant | Immobilization device | Project decision (not settled by the sources reviewed) |
| Distraction, preparation or play-specialist methods to avoid restraint | Relevant | Alternatives to restraint | RCN 2019 |
| Clinician discusses risks or complications of restraint in children | Relevant | Safety content | 2024 scoping reviews; RCN 2019 |
| Hospital policy, news story or opinion on restraint, with no technique | Unsure | Topic fits, content unclear | Project decision |
| Manikin or doll used to teach an infant holding technique | Relevant | Rule A.3 | Project decision |
| Age not stated and not judgeable | Unsure | Cannot test A | Project decision |

# 7. Language

All search queries are in English, but some videos are in other languages. Annotators do not need to speak the video's language.

1. Do not exclude a video because of its language. Judge the content.
2. Use machine translation for the title, description, hashtags and transcript, and use what is visible in the video (device, setting, child, demonstration).
3. If the translated text and the visuals together allow a decision on A, B and C, label normally.
4. If they do not, label **Unsure**, reason: language.
5. The platform language field is shown for information only and can be wrong.

# 8. Information shown, and video watching procedure

**Shown to the annotator:** platform, URL, title or caption, description, hashtags, transcript (when present, truncated if very long), creator name, duration, upload date, platform language.

**Hidden from the annotator:** query text, query ids, subtopic, number of queries that found the video, search rank, views, likes, comments, shares, saves, follower counts, any model score, and the other annotator's labels. This prevents the way a video was found, or its popularity, from influencing the label.

**Watching procedure.**

1. Read the title or caption, description, hashtags and transcript first.
2. Watch the video with sound on, or with captions.
   - Up to 3 minutes: watch fully.
   - 3 to 20 minutes: watch the first 60 seconds, then check at about 25%, 50% and 75% of the length, and any part the transcript points to.
   - Over 20 minutes: watch the first 2 minutes and the same three checkpoints.
3. Never label from the title, hashtags or thumbnail alone.
4. To label **Relevant**, you must have seen or heard at least one passage that meets condition C.
5. If the procedure leaves the decision open, label **Unsure** with a reason and a one-line note.

**Fields to fill:** label; conditions A, B, C (Yes, No or Unclear); unsure reason; unsafe demonstration flag; note.

# 9. Unsafe demonstration versus safety education

Videos about restraint sometimes show practices that guidelines advise against. The label and the safety flag are recorded separately.

| Situation | Label | Unsafe demonstration flag |
|---|---|---|
| The video teaches what to avoid or why a practice is risky (for example, why a device must not restrict the chest) | Relevant (safety education) | Yes |
| The video presents an unsafe practice as the way to do it (device over the chest or face, child left alone in a device, prolonged forceful pinning with no safety framing) | Irrelevant | Yes |
| The video shows a safe, guideline-consistent technique | Relevant | No |
| Forceful holding of a crying child, with no explanation and no safety content | Irrelevant | Yes if the practice is visibly unsafe |

**Test:** does the video present the practice as something to do, or as something to avoid? Unsafe means conflicting with the sources in section 2, such as airway or chest obstruction, an unattended child, or force beyond what is needed. The unsafe flag stays "No" when the practice is only unfamiliar.

# 10. Annotation plan

## Sample size

**Recommended: about 400 videos, 200 per platform (YouTube and TikTok). Minimum: 300.**

At 95% confidence, a margin of about plus or minus 5% needs roughly 385 labels. With 200 per platform, the margin per platform is about plus or minus 7%. With 300 in total, it is about plus or minus 5.7%. The count of the rarer class matters more than the total, so check the class balance after the pilot. If either class is below about 15% of the pilot, raise the main sample size before drawing it.

## Steps

| Step | What | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Freeze the data | Draw the samples only from the final cleaned file, after preprocessing is finished | none |
| 1. Pilot | Two annotators label the same videos independently. Every disagreement is discussed and this document is refined. Pilot videos are not reused in the main sample | 30 to 50 videos |
| 2. Main sample | Stratified random sample by platform and subtopic, so small subtopics (dental, wound care, neonatal) are represented | about 400 |
| 3. Double annotation | A second independent annotator labels a random subset. Cohen's kappa is reported | about 20%, about 80 videos |
| 4. Adjudication | A third person resolves disagreements and Unsure items | as needed |

**Pilot selection.** The pilot videos are chosen to be diverse: spread across both platforms and all subtopics, with and without transcripts, short and long, English and non-English. This makes the pilot test the hard cases and not only the typical ones.

**Pilot target.** Cohen's kappa of at least 0.70 on the three-label scheme (project target) before the main annotation starts. If it is lower, revise the criteria and repeat the pilot with a new set of videos.

## Disagreement and adjudication

- Both annotators label independently, without discussion, before agreement is computed.
- Items with a disagreement, and every item labeled Unsure by any annotator, go to a **third adjudicator**, who sees both labels and notes and sets the final label.
- The adjudicator should have clinical or pediatric-care background, or be the supervising researcher.
- Adjudicated labels are marked as such in the final file, so that agreement is always computed on the original labels.

## Annotator qualifications

Minimum requirements for every annotator and the adjudicator:

1. Reads English fluently.
2. Has read this document and passed the pilot (section above).
3. Has health, nursing, medical or related training, or has been trained by someone who has.
4. Labels without seeing any model output.

| Role | Name | Qualification | Training date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annotator A | | | |
| Annotator B | | | |
| Adjudicator | | | |

## Rules for annotators

- Use only the fields in section 8.
- Write a one-line note for every Unsure and every unsafe demonstration flag.
- Do not change earlier labels after seeing later videos, except through adjudication.

## Use of the labels

The labels train and evaluate the screening stage and set its thresholds for relevant, uncertain and irrelevant videos. Because the main sample is stratified and not proportional, the key file stores a sample weight for each video, to be used when estimating performance on the full dataset.

# 12. Limits

- No single guideline covers the whole topic. The criteria combine nursing, dental, sedation, vaccination, trauma and review sources, and the mapping from those sources to a video label is the project's own interpretation.
- The dental criteria rest on the published abstract of the AAPD document. They should be checked against its full text before the criteria are finalized.
- Several rows, such as limb splinting, mixed-content thresholds and the Unsure cases, are project decisions that the sources did not settle. They are marked as such and are tested in the pilot.
- Guidelines describe professional practice. Videos by parents or other creators may be sound without citing any guideline. The criteria judge what the video shows, not who made it.
- The kappa target of 0.70 and the 30-second mixed-content threshold are project choices.
- Sample-size figures assume simple random sampling. Stratification and clustering by creator change the margins slightly.
